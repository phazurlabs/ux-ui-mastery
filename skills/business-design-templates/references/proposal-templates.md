# Proposal Templates — Complete Design Proposal Library

## 1. Website / App Redesign Proposal

This is the most common design proposal type. The client has an existing digital product that is underperforming, outdated, or misaligned with current business goals. The proposal must demonstrate understanding of the current state, articulate what is wrong and why, and present a clear path to improvement with measurable outcomes.

### Cover Page

```
[YOUR LOGO]

─────────────────────────────────────────────

[CLIENT NAME]
Website Redesign Proposal

Prepared by: [Your Name / Agency Name]
Date: [Month Day, Year]
Valid through: [Date — typically 30 days]

─────────────────────────────────────────────

CONFIDENTIAL
This document contains proprietary methodology
and pricing. Distribution requires written consent.
```

### Executive Summary

```
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

[Client Name]'s current [website/application] is not performing to its
potential. Based on our initial assessment and conversations with your
team, we have identified [X] critical areas where design improvements
can directly impact your business metrics:

1. [Primary issue — e.g., "The current checkout flow has a 73%
   abandonment rate, significantly above the industry average of 48%"]
2. [Secondary issue — e.g., "Mobile users account for 64% of traffic
   but only 28% of conversions, indicating a mobile experience gap"]
3. [Tertiary issue — e.g., "Customer support receives approximately
   [X] tickets per month related to navigation confusion"]

We propose a [X]-phase redesign engagement that addresses these
challenges through user research, information architecture
restructuring, and responsive interface design.

Expected outcomes:
- [Outcome 1 with metric — e.g., "Reduce checkout abandonment by
  15-25 percentage points"]
- [Outcome 2 with metric — e.g., "Increase mobile conversion rate
  to parity with desktop within 6 months of launch"]
- [Outcome 3 with metric — e.g., "Decrease navigation-related
  support tickets by 40-60%"]

Proposed investment: $[XX,XXX] - $[XX,XXX]
Estimated timeline: [X] weeks
```

### Understanding Section

```
YOUR CURRENT SITUATION

[Write 2-3 paragraphs demonstrating deep understanding of the client's
business, their users, and the specific problems they are facing.
Reference specific data points from discovery calls, analytics shared
by the client, or your own preliminary assessment.]

[Client Name] serves [describe target audience] through [describe the
product/service]. Your current [website/application] was [last redesigned
in YEAR / built on PLATFORM / designed for a different business model].

Since then, several factors have created a gap between your digital
experience and your business needs:

Business Context:
- [Factor 1 — e.g., "Your product catalog has grown from 200 to 2,400
  SKUs, but the navigation structure was designed for the original catalog
  size"]
- [Factor 2 — e.g., "You have expanded into 3 new market segments, each
  with different user needs and purchase patterns"]
- [Factor 3 — e.g., "Mobile traffic has shifted from 35% to 64% of total
  visits since the last redesign"]

User Context:
- [User insight 1 — e.g., "Your primary users are [persona type] who
  prioritize [need] but currently struggle with [pain point]"]
- [User insight 2 — referencing any user feedback, support tickets, or
  behavioral data the client shared]

Competitive Context:
- [Competitive observation — e.g., "[Competitor A] launched a redesigned
  experience in [timeframe] that addresses similar user needs with
  [specific approach]"]
```

## 2. New Product Design Proposal

For greenfield projects where there is no existing product. The emphasis shifts from "fixing what is broken" to "validating the concept and building the right thing."

### Executive Summary (New Product Variant)

```
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

[Client Name] is entering the [market/space] with a new [product type]
targeting [audience]. The opportunity is significant: [market data point
— e.g., "The global [market] is projected to reach $[X]B by [year],
with [segment] growing at [X]% CAGR"].

However, [X]% of new digital products fail within their first [X] years,
primarily due to building features users do not need and neglecting
usability in the rush to market. A research-driven design process
reduces this risk by validating assumptions before committing to
development.

We propose a [X]-phase engagement that takes [Product Name] from concept
validation through production-ready design:

Phase 1: Concept Validation & User Research
Phase 2: Information Architecture & UX Design
Phase 3: Visual Design System & High-Fidelity UI
Phase 4: Prototype Testing & Iteration
Phase 5: Development Handoff & Design System Package

Expected outcomes:
- Validated product concept with evidence of user demand
- Complete UX architecture covering [X] core user flows
- Production-ready design system with [X]+ components
- Tested prototype with usability benchmarks established
- Developer-ready specification package

Proposed investment: $[XX,XXX] - $[XXX,XXX]
Estimated timeline: [X] weeks
```

### New Product Approach Section

```
OUR APPROACH: BUILD THE RIGHT THING, THEN BUILD IT RIGHT

New product design requires a fundamentally different approach than
redesign. There is no existing user behavior to analyze, no analytics
baseline to improve upon, and no established user expectations to honor.
Every assumption must be validated.

Phase 1: Concept Validation ([X] weeks)
───────────────────────────────────────
The goal of this phase is to answer one question: "Is this the right
product to build?"

Activities:
- Founder/stakeholder vision workshops ([X] sessions)
- Target user interviews ([X] sessions, recruited by [method])
- Jobs-to-Be-Done analysis
- Competitive landscape mapping (direct + indirect competitors)
- Value proposition canvas development
- Concept testing with low-fidelity prototypes ([X] participants)

Deliverables:
- Validated (or pivoted) product concept document
- User personas grounded in research (not assumptions)
- Jobs-to-Be-Done framework
- Competitive positioning map
- Feature prioritization matrix (must-have / should-have / could-have)
- Go/no-go recommendation with supporting evidence

Decision point: Concept validation results determine whether to
proceed as planned, pivot the approach, or de-scope.

Phase 2: UX Architecture ([X] weeks)
─────────────────────────────────────
Activities:
- User flow mapping for all core workflows
- Information architecture development
- Wireframe design ([X] key screens + [X] secondary screens)
- Rapid prototype for primary user flow
- Usability testing on wireframe prototype ([X] participants)
- Iteration based on test results

Phase 3: Visual Design System ([X] weeks)
─────────────────────────────────────────
Activities:
- Brand expression in UI (working within brand guidelines or
  developing UI-specific brand extensions)
- Design token architecture (color, typography, spacing, elevation)
- Component library design ([X]+ components, all states)
- Screen-level design for all [X] templates
- Responsive adaptation across [X] breakpoints
- Design system documentation

Phase 4: Prototype Testing ([X] weeks)
──────────────────────────────────────
Activities:
- High-fidelity interactive prototype
- Usability testing ([X] participants, [X] task scenarios)
- Desirability testing (Microsoft Desirability Toolkit or similar)
- Iteration based on findings
- Final design refinement

Phase 5: Handoff ([X] weeks)
────────────────────────────
Activities:
- Figma file architecture for Dev Mode
- Design token JSON export
- Component specification documentation
- Interaction annotation for all flows
- Developer walkthrough sessions ([X] sessions)
- Design QA process setup
```

## 3. Design System Creation Proposal

A specialized proposal for building a design system from scratch or formalizing an existing component library into a governed system.

### Executive Summary (Design System Variant)

```
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

[Client Name] currently maintains [X] digital products across [X]
platforms, built by [X] development teams. Without a unified design
system, each team makes independent decisions about components, patterns,
spacing, and interaction behaviors. The result:

- Visual inconsistency across products (users experience [X] different
  button styles, [X] different form patterns, [X] different navigation
  approaches)
- Developer inefficiency: each team rebuilds common components from
  scratch, spending an estimated [X] hours per [month/quarter] on
  duplicated effort
- Design debt: [X] unique color values, [X] font size variations,
  and [X] spacing values across the codebase (audit findings)
- Accessibility gaps: inconsistent ARIA patterns and keyboard navigation
  across products

We propose building a comprehensive design system that serves as the
single source of truth for [Client Name]'s digital products.

Expected outcomes:
- [X]% reduction in front-end component development time (industry
  benchmark: 30-50% after design system maturity)
- Visual consistency across all [X] products within [X] months
- Accessibility compliance (WCAG 2.2 AA) built into every component
- Designer onboarding time reduced from [X] weeks to [X] days
- Developer onboarding time reduced from [X] weeks to [X] days

Proposed investment: $[XXX,XXX] - $[XXX,XXX]
Estimated timeline: [X] months (MVP) + ongoing governance
```

### Design System Scope of Work

```
SCOPE OF WORK — DESIGN SYSTEM

Tier 1: Foundation (Month 1-2)
──────────────────────────────
□ Design principles documentation ([X] core principles)
□ Token architecture: color (primitive + semantic + component layers)
□ Token architecture: typography (type scale, weights, line heights)
□ Token architecture: spacing (base unit, scale, application rules)
□ Token architecture: elevation (shadow system, layering model)
□ Token architecture: motion (duration scale, easing curves)
□ Token architecture: border radius, border width, opacity
□ Grid system: [X] breakpoints with column/gutter/margin definitions
□ Iconography system: [X] core icons, grid standards, contribution guide
□ Figma variable setup: primitives, semantic modes (light/dark/HC)
□ Token export pipeline: Figma → JSON → Style Dictionary → platform code

Tier 2: Core Components (Month 2-4)
────────────────────────────────────
□ Button (primary, secondary, ghost, danger × 3 sizes × all states)
□ Input (text, number, email, password, search × all states)
□ Textarea (with character count, auto-resize option)
□ Select (single, multi, searchable, grouped)
□ Checkbox, Radio, Toggle (individual + group patterns)
□ Form layout patterns (vertical, horizontal, inline, multi-column)
□ Card (content card, action card, media card, stat card)
□ Modal / Dialog (alert, confirmation, form, full-screen)
□ Toast / Notification (success, error, warning, info × dismissible)
□ Navigation (top bar, sidebar, bottom tabs, breadcrumbs)
□ Table (sortable, filterable, selectable, expandable rows)
□ Pagination (numbered, load more, infinite scroll)
□ Avatar (image, initials, icon × sizes)
□ Badge (status, count, notification)
□ Tooltip / Popover
□ Tabs (horizontal, vertical, scrollable)
□ Accordion / Expandable section
□ Progress (bar, circular, steps)
□ Skeleton / Loading states
□ Empty states (no data, no results, error, first-time)

Tier 3: Patterns & Documentation (Month 4-5)
─────────────────────────────────────────────
□ Authentication flows (sign in, sign up, forgot password, MFA)
□ Search pattern (search bar, results, filters, no results)
□ Data entry patterns (multi-step forms, inline editing, bulk actions)
□ Dashboard layout patterns (metric cards, charts, activity feeds)
□ Settings/preferences pattern
□ User profile pattern
□ Onboarding flow pattern
□ Error handling patterns (form, page-level, network, permission)
□ Pattern documentation (when to use, anatomy, do/don't, code examples)
□ Contribution guidelines (how to propose new components/patterns)
□ Governance model (review process, versioning, deprecation policy)

Tier 4: Adoption & Enablement (Month 5-6)
──────────────────────────────────────────
□ Team training sessions ([X] sessions, [X] hours each)
□ Migration guide for existing products
□ Figma library setup with publishing and update workflow
□ Storybook integration documentation
□ Design review checklist (using the design system)
□ Adoption metrics dashboard setup
□ Quarterly review cadence established

NOT INCLUDED:
□ Code implementation of components (design specifications only —
  development team builds from specs, or add code package as separate SOW)
□ Content migration for existing products
□ Brand redesign (working within existing brand or specified direction)
□ Mobile app components beyond responsive web (native iOS/Android
  available as separate engagement)
```

## 4. UX Audit Proposal

Shorter engagement focused on evaluating an existing product and providing actionable recommendations.

### Executive Summary (UX Audit Variant)

```
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

[Client Name] has requested a comprehensive UX audit of [product name]
to identify usability issues, accessibility gaps, and opportunities for
improvement. This audit will provide a prioritized, actionable roadmap
for design improvements, each tied to expected business impact.

The audit covers:
- Heuristic evaluation against Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics
- Accessibility audit against WCAG 2.2 Level AA
- User flow analysis for [X] critical user journeys
- Competitive UX benchmarking against [X] competitors
- Analytics-informed analysis of drop-off points and friction

Deliverable: A comprehensive audit report with findings ranked by
severity and business impact, including specific design recommendations
and effort estimates for each fix.

Proposed investment: $[X,XXX] - $[XX,XXX]
Estimated timeline: [X] weeks
```

### UX Audit Scope of Work

```
SCOPE OF WORK — UX AUDIT

Week 1: Evaluation
────────────────────
□ Heuristic evaluation: All [X] core screens evaluated against
  Nielsen's 10 heuristics (see nng-ux-heuristics skill)
□ Accessibility scan: Automated (axe-core, WAVE) + manual
  keyboard/screen reader testing across [X] key flows
□ Analytics review: Identify top drop-off points, error-prone
  flows, and underperforming pages
□ Competitive teardown: [X] competitors evaluated on same criteria

Week 2: Analysis & Synthesis
─────────────────────────────
□ Issue categorization: Each finding scored on severity (critical /
  major / minor / cosmetic) and frequency (all users / many / few)
□ Impact mapping: Each issue linked to business metric impact
□ Opportunity identification: Quick wins vs. strategic improvements
□ Recommendation development: Specific, actionable fix for each issue

Week 3: Report & Presentation
──────────────────────────────
□ Comprehensive audit report ([X]+ pages)
□ Executive summary (1-page findings overview for leadership)
□ Prioritized roadmap with effort estimates
□ Presentation of findings to stakeholder group
□ Q&A session and next-steps discussion

AUDIT REPORT STRUCTURE:
1. Executive summary (1 page)
2. Methodology description
3. Findings by category (with screenshots and annotations)
   a. Usability issues (heuristic violations)
   b. Accessibility issues (WCAG failures)
   c. User flow friction points
   d. Competitive gaps
4. Prioritized recommendation matrix
   (Impact × Effort quadrant for each recommendation)
5. Quick wins (high impact, low effort — implement immediately)
6. Strategic improvements (high impact, high effort — plan for)
7. Detailed fix specifications for top [X] issues
8. Appendices (raw data, full heuristic evaluation sheets)
```
